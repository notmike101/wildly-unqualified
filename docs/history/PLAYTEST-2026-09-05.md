Date: 2026-09-05. The user considers this a successful rough-prototype gameplay test and requested a more polished MVP. This record distinguishes that acceptance from technical completion of the final exhibition step.

## Observed run

Four independent Edge profiles rendered visible WebGPU windows in a two-by-two layout. The coordinator played Rowan as host; three independently acting subagents played Fern, Ash and Reed and communicated about their actions. The session allowed four active agents including the coordinator, so this was not four child agents plus a fifth host.

Players used keyboard controls and actual UI buttons. Read-only world snapshots helped navigation and timing; this was a guided agent run, not an unfamiliar human duration test. No gameplay state or assignment credit was injected.

- Fern photographed the raccoon inspecting the bait tin, with Ash assisting.
- The same tin was stolen, followed to the pond, reclaimed and reused.
- Rowan photographed the heron displaying, with Ash assisting.
- Fern captured the combined portrait after Ash held the open tin near the stash. Reed took a backup photograph.
- All four JPEGs were ready. Two bait portions remained. All four players returned to camp; guest Ready actions are in their logs and all-four readiness was observed during the final attempt.
- The first logged confirmation of all assignments was at 466.8 simulation seconds (7:47). Rowan's return was logged at 588.4 seconds (9:48); setup and controller recovery time are excluded. These timings do not establish a 20–30-minute human outing.

The initial server/tunnel process interruption happened before the outing. The same saved room was restored on a new origin, and guests were reassigned through the host UI. Later, the browser-owner tool timed out during the exhibition attempt and closed its browsers. Reopening the same profiles preserved objectives and photos, but reconnection cleared readiness and paused the room. A Computer Use policy check then stopped the window inspection because it could not confidently determine the browser URL. Exhibition opening and favorite selection were not verified.

At the user's subsequent request, the four identified test browser instances were closed through their browser control connections. Their exact profile paths were checked, and all four control-port listeners were confirmed gone. Other browser windows and Blender were not closed.

## User observations and source checks

| User observation | What inspection establishes | MVP consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Minimal or absent collisions | `level.ts` supplies a small wall set; most props placed by `view.ts` have no corresponding movement/physics geometry. | Rendered placement, collision, navigation and photo occlusion must share authored world data. |
| Predictable NPC paths | `game.ts` cycles the raccoon through three fixed points and switches the heron between two perches. | Choose reachable goals from perception, local conditions and recent choices; preserve readable reactions. |
| No useful player colors or names in the world | `view.ts` already tints Outfit/Raincoat/Vest materials; inspected GLBs contain those names. Tinting alone did not meet the user's readability expectation. No overhead player-name implementation exists. | Stronger stable crew accents, names and non-color identifiers; visually verify all outfits under game lighting. |
| Very short loop | The three photo conditions were satisfied in under eight simulation minutes in this guided run; the held tin can maintain inspection. | Add distinct physical problems and behavior setups, not mandatory delays. Remove the unlimited inspection strategy. |
| Trees in paths | Tree exclusion in `view.ts` uses a line approximation rather than the actual trail segments and canopy footprints. The crew repositioned around a pine during the run. | Author paths first; validate trunk, canopy, handles, entrances and camera corridors against them. |
| Little environmental detail | Existing assets include 24 reserve props but placement and terrain are sparse; the ground is largely one flat slab. | Compose three distinct habitats with coherent detail, landmarks and traversable banks. |

The color finding is a visibility/design issue until a rendering defect is reproduced. Controller and policy interruptions are recorded separately from confirmed game bugs. No assertion is made that the final click timeout proves a specific layout defect; compact-window end controls require a fresh test.
