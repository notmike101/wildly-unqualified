# Wildly Unqualified: generated reserves and forest audio

Date: 2026-09-05. Status: **approved for implementation**, including the dense-forest/wildlife revision and the user's private GitHub/versioning requirement. Implementation progress is recorded separately.

The user requested a larger, more random reserve, procedural levels, randomized objectives, sounds and possibly peaceful music. They explicitly chose **40–60-minute outings**. This replaces the earlier 20–30-minute pacing target for this expansion. The mature low-poly forest, photography premise, regular physical cooperation, reversible mischief, two-to-four-player internet play and portable self-hosting remain requirements.

The user's next revision makes **dense forest the default** and expands both wildlife species and population. The revised content target is **12 species and 36–48 individual animals per reserve**, replacing the initial eight-animal proposal. These are planned deliverables; the new models and behavior have not been implemented.

## Recommendation and alternatives

Generate a finite forest from authored habitat modules, connected trails and seeded environmental placement. Select objectives against the completed map and its resident wildlife. Add a small native Web Audio soundscape and optional quiet music.

| Approach                                                           | Benefit                                                                                                                                                              | Limitation / decision                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Authored habitat modules with generated arrangement and routes** | Geography, approaches and assignments change while retaining reliable ground, equipment clearance and photographic compositions. Reuses the current art and physics. | Recommended. Module connections and objective prerequisites need validation.                               |
| Shuffle objectives and dressing on an enlarged fixed map           | Smallest code change.                                                                                                                                                | Does not deliver changing level layouts and retains the current predictable journey.                       |
| Unrestricted terrain noise, erosion or voxel generation            | Greater terrain freedom.                                                                                                                                             | Introduces terrain, navigation and physics work that the requested forest outing does not yet need. Defer. |

Use the existing TypeScript, Three.js, Box3D and Node stack. No new engine, procedural-generation library, online AI service, paid audio service or hosted dependency is needed.

## Evidence from the current game

Inspection found a fixed **156 × 136 m** world, 718 placements, 202 movement walls, 416 physics boxes, nine walkable surfaces and 28 navigation nodes. Its three trail polylines total approximately 365 m. The seed selects three binary habitat locations and three binary commission choices; it does not change the map layout. Sources: [level.ts](../../../src/shared/world/level.ts), [chooseOuting in encounters.ts](../../../src/server/simulation/wildlife/encounters.ts).

There are three animals, with identity equal to species, one global feeding patch, one gate and one crossing. Rendering, prediction, physics, navigation and photo validation import static world data. These assumptions must change together. [shared.ts](../../../src/shared/shared.ts), [game.ts](../../../src/server/simulation/game.ts), [save.ts](../../../src/server/persistence/save.ts).

The completed [MVP verification](../../history/VERIFICATION.md) records functional outing chains, but also two frequent observers during four-player photography and a dominant tin-escort solution. It does not establish human completion time or enjoyment. Expanding distances alone would preserve those weaknesses. The earlier [teamwork research](../../../../../research/wildly-unqualified-teamwork-and-mischief.md) supports persistent route preparation, rotating roles and recoverable interference as design hypotheses, not proven outcomes for this game.

Current audio is seven short oscillator effects in [view.ts](../../../src/client/rendering/view.ts), with a single volume control and distance attenuation in [main.ts](../../../src/client/main.ts). There are no natural sound beds or music. The distance calculation retains a small audible floor beyond its nominal range.

## The generated reserve

Start with an approximately **384 × 320 m envelope**, about 5.8 times the current area. This is a content and performance budget, not a mechanism for enforcing playtime. Place **eight main habitat areas**, a camp and **two small field stations at route junctions**. Each habitat supports several residents and encounter sites. Wildlife also uses the connecting forest, banks and canopy; eight habitat areas must not become eight isolated animal enclosures. Stations are equipment/rest areas within the route network.

Use coarse 64 m placement cells and quarter-turn habitat transforms so the existing axis-aligned banks, ramps and collision shapes remain practical. Select occupied cells, module variants and connections from the full seed. Reserve the connecting corridors before composing scenery. Connect the required destinations and add links that produce two useful loops with shortcuts back toward camp. An illustrative route is camp → first field loop → supply junction → second field loop → prepared return route; the particular destinations, branches and approaches change between seeds.

Each habitat module supplies local terrain, water boundaries, entrances, camera areas, cover, wildlife anchors and recovery points. Initial families are deep woodland/brook, canopy clearing and wooded wetland, using the existing forest kit. Their identity comes from composed trees, deadwood, rocks, water and sight lines. Vary which existing landmarks dominate a pocket: a hollow log, root arch, boulder stand, brook or reed bank. Keep the enclosing mature canopy and atmospheric sky.

**Density is a generated property of the whole reserve.** Target approximately **70% dense forest, 20% lighter woodland and 10% open ground**, measured over playable land excluding water. Allow roughly five percentage points of variation between seeds while keeping dense forest a clear majority. Open ground includes camp, larger clearings and exposed banks. Blend between broad stands instead of scattering alternating dense/open squares or leaving the spaces between habitats empty.

Dense stands need overlapping mature crowns, layered trunks, deadwood, roots and a varied forest floor. Keep the existing 18–32 m trees as the main visual mass. As an initial composition target, dense zones should have roughly 70–90% projected canopy coverage, confirmed by eye-height and upward views. Use irregular clusters and natural gaps; rows of evenly spaced trees, tiny ornamental trees and shrubs cannot supply this effect. Ground cover supports the trees and stays below useful camera sight lines where required.

Paths through dense stands remain beneath the canopy. The four-metre equipment clearance is a clear passage at ground/handling height, not a strip cleared to the sky. Let upper crowns bridge trails and use bends, trunks and terrain to break long corridor views. Preserve small deliberate camera windows and alternate approaches within dense habitats instead of clearing every animal's surroundings. Lighter woodland, occasional meadow-like clearings and water views provide contrast and expose the sky. Readable shade, crew accents, clues and animal silhouettes remain acceptance requirements.

Generation proceeds in this order:

1. Choose modules, their cells/orientations, the camp and station sites.
2. Connect entrances with traversable terrain and trails; add return loops and optional shortcuts. Intersecting paths have explicit junctions.
3. Place route fixtures, equipment, local wildlife anchors and photographic approach areas.
4. Populate residents and choose compatible objective instances.
5. Apply the connected density zones and scatter forest stands/floor detail outside reserved spaces, checking actual transformed bounds and canopy coverage.
6. Validate the complete level. Retry deterministically a bounded number of times; if every attempt fails, report creation failure and retain the previous outing. Never quietly open an invalid or unrelated map.

Guarantee a four-metre-wide equipment corridor and six metres of overhead clearance on designated carrying routes. Validate slopes, module seams, water edges, prop turning areas and the complete corridor, not just endpoint connectivity. Off-trail exploration remains possible where the terrain permits it; the trail network is the guaranteed usable route.

Every required pocket has an approach and a return route that remain possible with a closed gate or unprepared crossing. A planted tree must not block a required camera area, clue, handle or recovery point. Distant decorative canopy has no blocking trunk inside the playable reserve. Water surfaces remain visibly above their supporting ground, including restored photographs.

Reuse the 39-model forest/equipment library for vegetation, equipment and existing landmarks. Add **nine distinct animal models** for the expanded roster below, with species-specific silhouettes, poses, animation attachments, collision bounds and photographic subject points. Add the small habitat pieces those behaviors need: a beaver lodge/branch pile, a badger den entrance and squirrel cache detail. Branch/perch attachments and trunk feeding points should reuse the existing trees where possible. A texture or recolor does not count as a new animal model.

Compose identifiable forest stands before deciding which further vegetation silhouettes are missing. All new models are authored in **visible foreground Blender**, saved/exported to new versioned paths and checked at player scale. Preserve the existing scenes and exports; only claim Blender MCP use after exercising a real connection. Asset acceptance includes each animal performing its recognizable action in the dense production forest, not just a gallery render.

## A 40–60-minute outing

The required loop has a **40–52-minute design allocation**, with roughly 4–8 minutes of optional work for interested crews. These are starting allocations to validate, not measured predictions or countdowns.

| Part                           | Allocation | Crew decisions                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------ | ---------: | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Camp briefing and route choice |    3–4 min | Read commissions, compare branches and decide which equipment to take or cache.                           |
| First field loop               |  15–19 min | Follow evidence, prepare an approach, make photographs and open a useful route.                           |
| Field-station regroup          |    2–3 min | Refill and reposition equipment; choose the next branch. No compulsory rest timer.                        |
| Second field loop              |  15–19 min | Solve a different mix of observation and equipment problems, including the local shared-habitat portrait. |
| Return and exhibition          |    5–7 min | Use prepared shortcuts, gather at camp, review real photos and choose favorites.                          |

Generate **six required photo objectives and two optional objectives**, spread across at least five wildlife pockets. Requirements vary by subject, behavior, site, landmark and approach. Use this initial mix:

| Required category            | Concrete play                                                                                                                                                 | Existing foundation / added work                                                                               |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Two behavior studies         | Examples: a raccoon washing food, a squirrel caching a morsel, an otter grooming or a woodpecker tapping. Find the active place and frame the visible action. | Bind existing and new species routines to individual residents and local anchors.                              |
| One equipment-directed setup | Draw the commissioned resident toward a useful decoy or feeding setup while another player prepares cover/camera position.                                    | Reuse decoy, tin, bait and screen. Generate viable preparation positions instead of one global patch.          |
| One habitat composition      | Photograph the commissioned animal with a named, recognizable landmark in the same scene.                                                                     | Add a bounded visibility/framing predicate for the landmark, using existing geometric photo checks.            |
| One track-led passage study  | Follow fresh tracks and a directional clue, choose the active branch and photograph a deer, fox or badger using its passage.                                  | Add a short evidence-and-passage encounter using the selected species' rig, navigation and disturbance states. |
| One shared-habitat portrait  | Arrange a locally overlapping compatible pair and photograph both: raccoon/heron, deer/rabbit or beaver/mallard.                                              | Generalize the current combined portrait to validated local pairs, with suitable routines and setup positions. |

The passage encounter is the smallest new activity that breaks dependence on food placement. Provide several local passage anchors, short visible clue trails and a repeatable route. Disturbance changes the animal's local approach; it does not reset a multi-minute waiting timer. Tracks and visible behavior carry the same essential information as sound. Caption actual wildlife cues; generic decorative birds do not imply objective progress.

Select template parameters only after the level exists. Require **at least five species across the six required objectives**, including at least two of the nine added species, at least one useful task without the tin alongside a tin task, and at least five distinct required habitat/encounter areas. The full objective pool must support all 12 species; repeated outings cannot keep choosing only raccoon, deer and heron. Do not repeat an identical behavior template at the same resident. Distinct bindings prevent all commissions collapsing into one feeding setup; a clever photograph can still receive every credit it actually earns.

Expand the behavior-study pool to actions such as squirrel caching, woodpecker tapping, otter grooming, beaver branch work and owl roosting. A passage study can follow deer, fox or badger evidence. Shared-habitat portraits choose from authored compatible setups such as raccoon/heron, deer/rabbit or beaver/mallard, with locally overlapping routines and validated camera approaches. Ground, canopy and waterside subjects require different observation positions. Free photographs of every species remain available regardless of that outing's commissions and can be retained/favorited in the notebook.

The two optional objectives come from a small pool of crew cameos and recoverable field mishaps: wildlife with a teammate, an observable bait spill, or a borrowed hat. They never unlock a required route, consume a unique resource or require a prank to finish the outing. Their absence cannot delay exhibition.

Every objective lists its subject IDs, habitat/landmark anchors, relevant behavior, tool/resource prerequisites and required/optional status. Generation verifies compatible actors, renewable supplies, an approach, a camera position and a repeatable opportunity. A missing opportunity is a content error, not an instruction to wait indefinitely.

## Wildlife, teamwork and mischief

Deliver **12 wildlife species and 36–48 persistent, photographable individuals per reserve**. Every generated reserve includes the full roster, with seeded abundance, groups, home ranges and active encounter locations. Common small animals can occur in loose groups; larger or more elusive residents appear less frequently. Keep a hard maximum of 48 resident actors for this expansion and tune how they are distributed, rather than filling every clearing with a crowd. Decorative insects, sound-only birds and background silhouettes do not count toward this population.

The following are proposed stylized game routines, not an ecosystem simulation. Each species needs a different recognizable silhouette and at least one distinctive observable action or response that can support photography:

| Species            | Habitat and observable game routine                                                         | Photo/setup variety                                                             |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Raccoon — existing | Woodland and banks; inspect food, wash it, borrow a hat and retreat locally.                | Existing tin/wash setups and brief causal mischief.                             |
| Deer — existing    | Woodland edges and lighter stands; graze, scan, move in a loose group and follow a passage. | Quiet approach, decoy placement and passage framing.                            |
| Heron — existing   | Wooded wetland; wade, feed, preen and display.                                              | Waterside sight lines, quiet feeding and paired portraits.                      |
| Red fox — new      | Woodland margins; listen toward a rustle, stalk and make a short pounce.                    | Follow evidence and anticipate an action from cover.                            |
| Rabbit — new       | Small light patches and sheltered edges; nibble, freeze and bound back to cover.            | Low camera positions, patient group portraits and careful approach.             |
| Squirrel — new     | Forest floor and authored tree anchors; forage, climb and cache a morsel.                   | Follow movement between ground and trunk; photograph caching.                   |
| Beaver — new       | Brook/pond banks; gnaw a branch and carry it along a short route to its lodge.              | Frame branch work and bank-to-water routines. No terrain destruction.           |
| River otter — new  | Water and bank anchors; swim, surface, roll or groom on a resting bank.                     | Anticipate surfacing or take a clear bank portrait.                             |
| Badger — new       | Dense ground cover and a den mouth; sniff, paw at a forage patch and retreat to cover.      | Read ground evidence and arrange a patient low-angle shot.                      |
| Owl — new          | Shaded high perches; roost, turn its head and make short perch-to-perch flights.            | Find an overhead subject and a gap through the canopy. No night cycle required. |
| Woodpecker — new   | Mature trunks and snags; cling, tap and flutter to another authored trunk point.            | Locate a visible/tapped clue and frame the trunk-facing pose.                   |
| Mallard — new      | Shallow wetland and exposed banks; paddle in a loose group, forage and preen.               | Water/shore compositions and a compatible shared-habitat portrait.              |

Every individual has an instance ID, home range, routine/retreat anchors and independent seeded choices. Reuse common movement, perception, cooldown and recovery helpers while keeping species actions and interests explicit. Ground animals use the ground graph; climbing/perched and swimming species use bounded authored links/anchors in the same world blueprint. Validate these links against trunks, canopy, water and banks. Short flight/climb/swim routines do not require unrestricted aerial navigation or a general ecosystem engine.

Keep residents distributed through the habitat areas and their connecting stands. Use tracks, disturbed vegetation, calls and visible movement to make the denser forest discoverable. Animals remain present and individually saved when away from the crew. Detailed perception/path decisions can run less often in distant regions, but resume from their own state; do not replace off-screen residents with random new animals. Nearby movement and capture state remain server-authoritative. Bound lure travel locally, keep supported portrait pairs close, and avoid giving every species the same food-following response.

Keep one shared tin, field case, screen and decoy. Each generated washout that accepts a permanent plank has its own nearby plank. Initially use two such washouts plus gate/bank/cover arrangements; each has a usable detour. A seated plank stays useful rather than becoming equipment that must be repeatedly reclaimed for another mandatory crossing.

On each field loop, offer two nearby opportunities with different tools: for example, a tin/photo pair can work while a second pair scouts a passage, carries the decoy or prepares the next approach. Two players can do the same work sequentially. All players retain the same capabilities. Do not add classes, four-person switches or fixed porter roles. Keep useful tasks close enough for players to rejoin without another expedition.

Field stations provide renewable refill access, safe equipment staging and local recovery anchors. A minor spill or stuck prop should invite a short local response. Recovery uses a reachable safe point, not the geometrically nearest point on the opposite side of an obstacle. Recover cannot become arbitrary fast travel for held gear. Reconnect retains a safe last position, with a visited station or camp fallback; it does not automatically send a returning player across the whole reserve. Clear stale grips and inputs as today.

Mischief remains causal: leave the case open, place a tempting decoy badly, rattle a tin beside a quiet subject or let a curious raccoon approach a hat. More animals must not multiply harassment. Retain one active hat incident, short recovery, repeat protection and bounded spills. Completed objectives and saved photographs survive every mishap. Opportunities should recur through ordinary equipment use; do not schedule guaranteed sabotage or inflate recovery time to fill the outing.

## Shared world and portable persistence

Introduce one immutable, resolved **world blueprint** per outing. It contains a world ID, seed, content/generator version, module transforms, bounds, placements, walkable and water geometry, trails/navigation, route fixture definitions, resident/anchor definitions, objective instances, and recovery/station locations. Save the accepted result; a future generator must never reinterpret an existing outing from its seed alone.

Include the density zones, complete 36–48-animal roster, home/group membership and ground/perch/water anchors. Species templates provide their own subject points and pose bounds so photo validation does not inherit a deer-sized proxy for a rabbit or bird. Validate the full expanded state and frozen captures against those definitions; persist all residents, including those currently far from players.

Keep static model/prop definitions and existing geometric helpers in `level.ts`. Add a focused `world.ts` for blueprint generation and validation. Pass the outing's world explicitly into rendering, collision/prediction, physics, navigation and photo checks. There is no mutable module-global current seed. Navigation caches must belong to the world instance and relevant route state so two different worlds cannot share cached paths accidentally.

Change only the identities that require it: animals separate ID from species; tin and hats identify the actual animal carrier; feeding portions are keyed by patch; gates/crossings are keyed by fixture; objective progress and photo credit are keyed by objective instance. Property counts, references and bounds are validated against the blueprint. Assistance credit relates to the photographed subjects/setup, rather than unrelated work near any animal.

Deliver the blueprint once on authenticated join or an outing change, with a content version and digest. Regular snapshots carry its identity and mutable state. The client waits for the matching world before rendering/predicting; an incompatible client receives a clear refresh/version error. Do not put thousands of static placements into the ten-times-per-second snapshot. The server remains authoritative for movement, interactions and photo credit.

Scope gameplay commands and cue IDs to the outing ID. Reject delayed input/actions from the preceding outing, and namespace photo IDs so an old upload cannot attach to a new run's photograph. Pause mutation/admission during the transition, drain the old save queue and install the new run only after its durable write succeeds. Existing authenticated room identity is separate from world identity.

Frozen photo frames retain their original world identity and all capture-time actor/equipment/route state. Store the blueprint once alongside the run, not once per pending image. Both current and restored capture rendering use that exact geometry. Uploaded JPEGs remain presentation data and cannot award credits.

Use save schema 3 and a new content version, `forest-expedition-1`, in a **new private data directory**. Preserve the current MVP release and saves intact; no automatic coordinate conversion or live-room replacement. Retain serialized atomic writes, valid backups, strict versions, admission and host recovery. Moving to another machine remains a copy of the portable release plus the complete private data directory, with the destination's port and origin configured. The inaccessible home server is not considered tested.

Reconcile photo retention before increasing objective counts. The current runtime retains all credited photos plus 21 extras while validation permits only 24 photos. For the expansion, use one shared **64-photo total limit** across runtime, validation and UI, reserve room for the eight possible objective-credit photos, and protect credited/favorited images from automatic eviction. Evict only the oldest uncredited, unfavorited extra. If the optional-photo allocation contains only favorites, a new uncredited shot can be previewed but the UI clearly reports that it was not retained until space is freed. Provide deletion/download for uncredited extras; never delete earned objective evidence to free space. Enforce the same 64 KiB JPEG limit and an 8 MiB save limit, with a 1 MiB maximum resolved blueprint. Reference the blueprint from captures and test the worst permitted combination rather than assuming these budgets fit.

Add a host-only **Next reserve** action after exhibition. Save and archive the completed run and its photos inside the private data folder, then atomically install a validated fresh outing. Keep room credentials and reserved crew identities; send the new world to connected clients and begin paused at camp. Refuse the transition while a capture is pending or if archive/save/generation fails. Leave the old outing usable on failure. Archive names include an outing ID, never overwrite an earlier archive, and moving the whole private directory preserves them. A campaign or searchable cross-outing gallery is outside this expansion.

Make retries idempotent: if the completed archive already exists after a failed transition, validate that it matches the same outing/content before reusing it; a mismatched archive is an error. A process interruption leaves either the valid old run or the durably installed new run, with the completed outing preserved.

## Forest sound and peaceful music

Use native Web Audio: one context unlocked by a Join/Continue user gesture, master gain and separate effects, ambience and music buses. Cache a small set of decoded effects, play positional events from their authoritative source, and update the listener from the actual camera. Replace the old distance multiplier instead of stacking two attenuation curves. Keep camera/UI feedback local. Current cue IDs already support deduplication. [MDN best practices](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Audio_API/Best_practices), [PannerNode](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/PannerNode), [looped audio buffers](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioBufferSourceNode/loop).

The initial palette is distinct shutter/whistle/tin/wood sounds, a few nonconsecutive footstep variants for trail, timber and wet ground, sparse background birds, local water and restrained wind/canopy ambience. Support the expanded roster with characteristic action sounds and suitable calls where useful: woodpecker taps, beaver gnawing, squirrel rustles and otter splashes can distinguish activity without every animal constantly vocalizing. Wildlife warnings and clue sounds must be recognizably different and tied to real actors. Generic bird ambience cannot stand in for an identified owl or woodpecker event. Add actual impact material/source metadata where available rather than guessing from a nearby model.

Keep music optional and initially off, with a plainly labeled control in settings. Include one or two quiet, sparse cues if they pass audition; allow stretches of silence and fade music below useful wildlife signals and camera work. The task does not require composing an hour-long score. Use short CC0 material or original synthesis/recordings with saved source files; do not imitate a commercial game's soundtrack.

Their titles do not establish sound quality. |

CC0 is a public-domain dedication to the extent permitted by law and permits commercial reuse; keep the exact license and provenance record for each imported file. [Creative Commons deed](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/). Record creator/title, primary/download URLs, license evidence, acquisition date, source/delivery hashes, edits and loop points. For original material, keep its source project and any sample dependencies. Generate visible voluntary audio credits from the same small manifest. The [audio research audit](../../../../.artifacts/wildly-unqualified/mvp-2026-09-05/next-audio-research-design.md) records candidate download checks and provenance limitations.

Start with a cap of 12 simultaneous effects and at most two ambience beds during a crossfade. Keep decoded short audio within a proposed 32 MiB budget. Fade/suspend world audio on pause, disconnect or a hidden page; returning starts one set of loops without replaying missed events. Master mute is absolute, music can remain disabled separately, and missing/blocked audio does not prevent play. Preserve captions and visible cues. Load audio from the portable game release, with no external playback service.
